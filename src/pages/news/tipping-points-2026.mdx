Modelling Gondwana dispersal quantitatively using ocean-floor images from satellite altimetry has provided many challenges.  Not least has been an internally consistent solution for the process of ocean creation around the Bouvet triple junction in the Late Jurassic and Early Crertaceous.  The key to success has been understanding the growth of the Weddell Sea in this time interval.  An **Antarctica-South America Corridor** has been identified in the ocean-floor topography that controls the relative pace of Africa and South America, particularly through the Cretaceous Quiet Zone, and hence closing the plate cicuit credibly.  Playing out *what-iff* scenarios in the Atlas software and refining solutions iteratively is tedious and time consuming.  What may appear as an open-ended problem inviting 'blue sky thinking' is in fact constrained by data and what is geometricallt possible - and plausible in animation.
